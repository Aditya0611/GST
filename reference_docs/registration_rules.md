# GST Registration Rules & GSTIN Structures

This document outlines the rules for compulsory GST registration and the format of the 15-character Goods and Services Tax Identification Number (GSTIN).

## 1. Threshold Limits for Registration
A business must register for GST when its aggregate turnover in a financial year exceeds the following thresholds:
*   **Supplier of Goods Only:** 
    *   Normal Category States: Rs. 40 Lakhs.
    *   Special Category States (NE states, Uttarakhand): Rs. 20 Lakhs.
*   **Supplier of Services / Mixed Supplies:**
    *   Normal Category States: Rs. 20 Lakhs.
    *   Special Category / Hilly States: Rs. 10 Lakhs.

## 2. Compulsory Registration (No Threshold Applies)
Certain businesses must register for GST regardless of their turnover:
*   Persons making any inter-state taxable supply of goods.
*   Casual Taxable Persons (CTP) making taxable supply.
*   Non-Resident Taxable Persons (NRTP) making taxable supply.
*   E-commerce operators and suppliers selling through e-commerce operators.
*   Persons liable to pay tax under Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM).

## 3. GSTIN Structure (15 Characters)
A GSTIN is a unique identifier assigned to every registered taxpayer:
*   **Characters 1-2 (State Code):** The 2-digit numerical prefix represents the state of registration (e.g., 27 for Maharashtra, 09 for Uttar Pradesh, 19 for West Bengal, 33 for Tamil Nadu, 07 for Delhi).
*   **Characters 3-12 (PAN):** The next 10 characters contain the taxpayer's Permanent Account Number (PAN).
*   **Character 13 (Entity Code):** Represents the number of registrations the entity has within the same state (usually 1, 2, etc., up to Z).
*   **Character 14 (Checksum Blank):** Typically the letter 'Z'.
*   **Character 15 (Checksum Digit):** Alphanumeric checksum character.

Example GSTIN: `27AAAAA0000A1Z5`
- State Code: `27`
- PAN: `AAAAA0000A`
- Entity registration count: `1`
- Fixed Character: `Z`
- Checksum: `5`
