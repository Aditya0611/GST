# GST on Exports and Imports

## Exports Under GST — Zero-Rated Supply

All exports of goods and services are treated as **zero-rated supplies** under Section 16 of the IGST Act. This means:
*   No GST is charged on exports.
*   The exporter can claim a **refund** of all ITC used in making those exports.

## Exports of Goods — Two Options

### Option 1: Export Under Bond / Letter of Undertaking (LUT) — No IGST Paid
*   Exporter files a **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** in Form RFD-11 at the beginning of each financial year.
*   Goods are exported **without payment of IGST**.
*   Exporter can claim refund of accumulated ITC on inputs used in the export.
*   **This is the most popular and preferred option** as it avoids cash flow blockage.

### Option 2: Export on Payment of IGST
*   Exporter pays IGST on the value of goods exported.
*   After goods depart India (confirmed by shipping bill and customs data), the exporter can claim a refund of the IGST paid.
*   Refund is processed automatically by GSTN based on shipping bill data from ICEGATE (customs system).

## Exports of Services — Conditions for Zero-Rating

A supply of service qualifies as **export of services** only if ALL of the following conditions are met (Section 2(6), IGST Act):
1.  The supplier of service is located in India.
2.  The recipient of service is located outside India.
3.  The place of supply of the service is outside India.
4.  The payment for the service is received in **convertible foreign exchange** (or in Indian Rupees where permitted by RBI).
5.  The supplier and recipient are **not merely establishments of a distinct person** (i.e., not just a branch of the same company).

## Export Invoice Requirements

*   Invoice must be in Indian Rupees or foreign currency.
*   Invoice must mention: "Supply meant for Export on payment of IGST" or "Supply meant for Export under Bond/LUT without payment of IGST".
*   Shipping Bill Number and Date must be mentioned (for goods).
*   Foreign currency invoices must show equivalent INR value.

## Refund of ITC for Exports (LUT Route)

Refund can be claimed for:
*   **Accumulated ITC** on account of exports (where output is zero-rated but inputs carry GST).
*   Refund is filed in **Form RFD-01**.
*   Refund is processed within **60 days** of filing a complete application.
*   Provisional refund of **90%** can be granted within 7 days for eligible exporters.

## Deemed Exports

Certain domestic supplies are treated as **deemed exports** and are eligible for zero-rating/refund:
*   Supply to Export Oriented Units (EOUs), Software Technology Parks (STPs), Electronic Hardware Technology Parks (EHTPs).
*   Supply to projects funded by international organizations (UN, World Bank, etc.) under specified conditions.
*   Supply of gold by a bank/PSU under Gold Monetization Scheme.

## SEZ (Special Economic Zone) Supplies

*   Supplies to **SEZ units or SEZ developers** are treated as **inter-state zero-rated supplies**.
*   SEZ supplies require an LUT or payment of IGST.
*   B2B supplies to SEZ are reported in GSTR-1 as SEZ supplies (separate tables).

## Imports Under GST — Overview

### Import of Goods
*   IGST is levied on the **transaction value + customs duty + landing charges**.
*   IGST on imports is collected by **Customs** at the port of entry.
*   The importer can claim IGST paid on imports as **ITC** (subject to normal ITC eligibility conditions).
*   Customs Duty (Basic Customs Duty, SWS) is **not creditable** as ITC — only IGST is.
*   **Bill of Entry** is the document evidencing import and ITC eligibility.

### Import of Services — Always RCM
*   IGST on import of services is paid by the **Indian recipient under RCM** (Reverse Charge).
*   Applicable even if the foreign supplier is not registered in India.
*   Includes: software subscriptions (SaaS), consulting fees, cloud services, digital advertising paid to foreign entities.
*   Self-invoice must be issued by the Indian recipient.
*   ITC of the RCM IGST can be claimed by the recipient in the **same tax period**.

### High Sea Sales
*   Sale of goods while they are in transit on the high seas (before customs clearance in India).
*   **Not subject to GST** (only customs duties apply).
*   The last buyer before customs clearance pays customs duty + IGST.

### Customs Bonded Warehouse Sales
*   Goods stored in a bonded warehouse before customs clearance can be sold.
*   GST (IGST) is applicable on such sales.

## Key Documents for Import ITC

To claim ITC on imported goods, the taxpayer must have:
*   **Bill of Entry** (with customs GSTIN)
*   Proof of payment of IGST to customs
*   Goods must have been received and used for business purposes
